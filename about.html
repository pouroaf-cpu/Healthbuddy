What Is Testosterone Replacement Therapy (TRT)?

TRT is a medically supervised treatment for clinically low testosterone. The goal is to restore normal function (energy, mood, libido, sleep, recovery) — not to chase bodybuilding doses.

Most TRT uses injectable testosterone attached to an ester. The testosterone is the hormone; the ester mainly controls how quickly it releases.

InjectBuddy helps you understand dosing math and planning. It does not replace clinical care.

Key Testosterone Esters Explained

Esters change the release speed. The hormone is the same — the ester changes how it absorbs, peaks, and tapers.

Testosterone Cypionate

Long-acting, smooth, commonly used in TRT. Often split into 1–2 injections per week (or more for steadier levels).

Testosterone Enanthate

Very similar to cypionate. Slightly shorter release profile, often treated as interchangeable in practice.

Testosterone Propionate

Short-acting, faster peaks and quicker clearance. Typically requires more frequent injections.

Blends & Long-Acting Options

Blends combine esters for different release profiles. Undecanoate is extremely long-acting and is commonly administered in a clinical setting.

Why Dosing and Frequency Matter

The same weekly dose behaves very differently depending on how it’s split. Large, infrequent injections tend to create bigger swings; smaller, more frequent doses typically smooth things out.

  • Shorter esters generally benefit from more frequent injections.
  • Longer esters can allow less frequent dosing, but planning still matters.
  • Symptoms and lab work should guide decisions — not guesswork.
