What is Testosterone Replacement Therapy (TRT)?

TRT is a medical treatment designed to restore testosterone levels in men who have been diagnosed with low or deficient testosterone. The goal is to bring levels back into a healthy, balanced range to improve mood, energy, libido, muscle mass, and overall well-being.

Therapy typically involves scheduled injections of testosterone, using an ester-based form of the hormone that controls how long it stays active in the body.

Understanding Testosterone Esters

Testosterone esters differ in how quickly they release testosterone into the bloodstream. The ester type determines injection frequency, stability, and how smooth hormone levels remain between doses.

Testosterone Cypionate

One of the most common forms used in North America. Cypionate has a longer half-life (around 7–8 days), meaning injections are often scheduled once or twice per week. It provides a stable, gradual release and is well-suited for long-term therapy.

Testosterone Enanthate

Nearly identical to cypionate, enanthate has a half-life of about 5–7 days. It’s widely used internationally and behaves almost the same pharmacologically, with slight differences in release rate and carrier oil.

Testosterone Propionate

A faster-acting ester with a half-life of about 2–3 days. Propionate is injected more frequently (every other day or three times a week). It reaches steady levels quickly but can cause more injection site irritation due to its shorter action.

Blends and Alternatives

Some preparations mix multiple esters (like Sustanon®) to provide both fast and slow release phases. Others may use undecanoate for extremely long duration (lasting up to 10–12 weeks per injection), though these are less common for self-managed therapy.

TRT Goals and Monitoring

Effective TRT isn’t about maximizing testosterone; it’s about maintaining balance. Most protocols aim for stable levels in the mid-to-upper physiological range, reducing mood swings and minimizing side effects. Blood work is essential to monitor total testosterone, free testosterone, estradiol, hematocrit, and lipid profiles.

  • Typical injection schedules range from 1–3 times per week depending on ester and dose.
  • Smaller, more frequent doses help maintain stability and reduce peaks/troughs.
  • Individual response varies; always follow clinical guidance when adjusting therapy.
