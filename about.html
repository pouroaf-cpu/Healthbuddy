What Is Testosterone Replacement Therapy (TRT)?

TRT is a medically supervised treatment for clinically low testosterone. Its purpose is to restore normal function — energy, mood, libido, sleep, and recovery — not to chase bodybuilding doses.

Most TRT uses injectable testosterone attached to an ester, which controls how slowly the hormone is released into the body.

InjectBuddy helps you understand your dosing math. It does not replace clinical care.

Key Testosterone Esters Explained

Different esters release testosterone at different speeds. The hormone is the same — the ester changes how quickly it absorbs, peaks, and tapers.

Testosterone Cypionate

Long-acting, smooth, commonly used in TRT. Often 1–2 injections per week.

Testosterone Enanthate

Very similar to cypionate. Slightly shorter half-life, often interchangeable.

Testosterone Propionate

Short-acting, faster peaks and clears. Usually requires more frequent injections.

Blends & Long-Acting Options

Blends combine esters for different release profiles. Undecanoate is extremely long-acting and generally administered in a clinical setting.

Why Dosing and Frequency Matter

The same weekly dose can behave very differently depending on how it’s split. Larger, infrequent injections produce bigger hormone swings; smaller, more frequent doses create smoother levels.

  • Shorter esters benefit from frequent injections.
  • Longer esters allow less frequent dosing but require planning.
  • Symptoms and lab work should guide all decisions.
