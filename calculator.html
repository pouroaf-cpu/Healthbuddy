Testosterone dosage calculator FAQ

A few common questions about how this calculator works and how to use the numbers safely.

The math here is purely mg ↔ mL based on vial strength, so it works the same for testosterone cypionate, enanthate, or other oil-based depot preparations, as long as you enter the correct mg/mL value from your vial. Pharmacology, half-life, and clinical decisions still sit with your prescriber.
This calculator is for planning and education only. It helps you translate a prescribed milligram dose into mL and visualise how it sits in a syringe. Your actual dose, schedule, and syringe choice should always follow your prescriber’s instructions, not this tool.
Vial strength is how concentrated the testosterone is in the vial. For example, a 200 mg/mL vial contains 200 mg of testosterone in every 1 mL of oil. If the vial is 250 mg/mL, the same mL volume will contain more testosterone.
Some clinicians prescribe a fixed number of injections per week (for example twice weekly), while others prefer “every X days” (for example every 3.5 days). The per-week mode is best if your script is written as “X injections each week”. The every-N-days mode is helpful if you cycle injections around a regular pattern like every 3 or 4 days.
Different syringe sizes change how “spread out” the markings are. A smaller syringe (for example 0.3 mL or 0.5 mL) gives finer control over small volumes. A larger syringe (1–3 mL) can hold more oil but is less granular at the low end. The calculator flags when your volume exceeds the selected syringe capacity and suggests when a smaller syringe could give better control.
Not all “insulin” syringes are the same. U-100 syringes are 0.01 mL per unit, but some syringes and regions use different unit scales. If you assume all units are identical when they are not, your actual volume (and therefore dose) will be off. Always confirm the unit-to-mL conversion for your specific syringe.
