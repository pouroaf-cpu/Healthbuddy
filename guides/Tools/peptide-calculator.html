What this calculator actually does

The peptide calculator converts a chosen dose (mcg or mg) into a syringe volume based on vial strength and reconstitution volume.

It does not decide dosing, timing, or suitability. Those decisions rely on bloodwork, goals, and overall hormone context.

Before you even touch a calculator

Dose math only makes sense when you understand your baseline. That starts with bloodwork.

  • Pre-injection bloodwork checklist
  • Bloodwork basics for TRT & peptides
  • How to read common lab markers

Ongoing use also requires regular follow-up labs to confirm stability.

Step 1: Enter peptide vial strength

Enter the total amount of peptide in the vial before reconstitution (for example, 5 mg or 10 mg).

  • This value comes directly from the vial label
  • It is not your per-dose amount
Common error

Confusing vial strength with dose size.

Step 2: Set reconstitution volume

Enter how much bacteriostatic water you added to the vial.

  • Example: 2 mL or 3 mL
  • This defines the final concentration

If you’re unsure, read peptide reconstitution basics before continuing.

Step 3: Enter your desired dose

Input the amount you intend to inject per dose.

  • Usually entered in micrograms (mcg)
  • The calculator converts this to liquid volume
Rule

mcg vs mg errors cause most overdoses.

Step 4: Review syringe volume

The output shows how much liquid to draw into the syringe for each injection.

  • Match the result to your syringe markings
  • Visually confirm before injecting

What the calculator does NOT do

  • Recommend peptide protocols
  • Adjust for peptide stability or degradation
  • Account for injection frequency strategies

Frequency considerations are covered separately in weekly vs every-N-days injection planning .
