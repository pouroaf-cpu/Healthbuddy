What this calculator actually does

The peptide calculator converts a desired dose (in micrograms or milligrams) into a syringe volume based on vial strength and reconstitution volume.

It does not decide what dose you should use—it makes the math accurate once a dose is chosen.

Step 1: Enter peptide vial strength

Enter the total amount of peptide in the vial before reconstitution (for example, 5 mg or 10 mg).

  • This value comes from the vial label
  • Do not enter your dose here
Common error

Confusing vial strength with per-dose amount.

Step 2: Set reconstitution volume

Enter how much bacteriostatic water you added to the vial.

  • Example: 2 mL or 3 mL
  • This determines peptide concentration per mL

For deeper context, see peptide reconstitution basics .

Step 3: Enter your desired dose

Input the amount of peptide you intend to inject per dose.

  • Usually entered in micrograms (mcg)
  • The calculator converts this to liquid volume
Rule

Always double-check units—mcg vs mg errors are common.

Step 4: Review syringe volume

The output shows how much liquid to draw into the syringe for each injection.

  • Match the result to your syringe type
  • Confirm markings visually before injecting

What the calculator does NOT do

  • It does not recommend peptide protocols
  • It does not adjust for peptide stability
  • It does not account for degradation or storage errors
  • It does not replace clinical guidance

Common peptide calculator mistakes

  • Mixing up mcg and mg
  • Forgetting how much water was added
  • Using the wrong syringe scale
  • Assuming more peptide equals better results
Pattern

Most peptide overdoses come from unit confusion.

Key takeaways

  • The calculator converts dose—it doesn’t choose it.
  • Vial strength and reconstitution volume matter equally.
  • Unit mistakes cause the biggest errors.
  • If the volume looks wrong, recheck inputs.
