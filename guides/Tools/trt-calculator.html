What this calculator actually does

The TRT dosage calculator converts a weekly testosterone dose into per-injection volume based on concentration and injection frequency.

It does not decide what dose you should be on—it makes the math accurate once a dose is chosen.

Step 1: Set your testosterone concentration

Enter the concentration printed on your vial (for example, 250 mg/mL).

  • This value never changes unless you switch products
  • Do not enter total vial amount
Common error

Entering total mg in the vial instead of mg per mL.

Step 2: Enter your total weekly dose

This is the total amount of testosterone you intend to use per week, regardless of how many injections you split it into.

  • Example: 150 mg per week
  • The calculator divides this automatically

Step 3: Choose injection frequency

Select how often you inject: weekly, twice weekly, every N days, or daily.

  • More frequent injections = smaller per-injection volume
  • Frequency affects stability, not total weekly dose

For more detail, see weekly vs every-N-days dosing .

Step 4: Review per-injection volume

The calculator outputs how much liquid to inject each time, shown in mL and syringe units.

  • Match this to your syringe type
  • Confirm markings visually before injecting
Rule

If the volume looks unusually large or small, recheck inputs.

What the calculator does NOT do

  • It does not recommend a dose
  • It does not adjust for bloodwork
  • It does not account for absorption differences
  • It does not replace clinical guidance

Common TRT calculator mistakes

  • Confusing mg with mL
  • Changing frequency but forgetting to recheck volume
  • Using the wrong syringe scale
  • Assuming higher dose equals better results
Pattern

Most “bad TRT” outcomes start with bad math.

Key takeaways

  • The calculator converts dose—it doesn’t choose it.
  • Correct concentration input is critical.
  • Frequency changes volume, not weekly total.
  • If the output surprises you, your input is probably wrong.
