Peptide Reconstitution FAQ

For planning only — not medical advice.

What is peptide reconstitution?

Most peptides arrive as lyophilized (freeze-dried) powder. Reconstitution means adding a diluent (commonly bacteriostatic water) to dissolve the powder so it can be drawn into a syringe.

What is bacteriostatic water?

Bacteriostatic water (BaW) is sterile water containing 0.9% benzyl alcohol. The preservative inhibits bacterial growth in multi-dose vials, allowing repeated withdrawals for up to 28 days once opened (if stored correctly). It is not the same as sterile water for injection (SWFI), which lacks a preservative.

How much bacteriostatic water should I add?

It depends on the concentration you want. Example: 5 mg peptide + 2 mL BaW → 2.5 mg/mL (2500 µg/mL). Use the calculator above to target a per-mL strength that makes dosing simple.

How do I calculate my draw volume after reconstitution?

Volume (mL) = Desired dose (mg) ÷ Vial strength (mg/mL).
Example: If vial strength is 2.5 mg/mL and you want 0.5 mg: 0.5 ÷ 2.5 = 0.20 mL.

Best practice to mix without damaging the peptide?
  • Swab the stopper and your work area.
  • Angle the vial and let BaW run gently down the glass; avoid blasting the powder.
  • Let dissolve passively; swirl gently. Do not shake hard or foam.
My solution is cloudy or won’t dissolve—what now?

Warm the vial in clean hands for 1–2 minutes and swirl gently. If particulates persist or the solution stays cloudy/discolored, do not use—contact your supplier/clinician.

Storage after reconstitution
  • Refrigerate (2–8 °C / 36–46 °F) unless your product instructions state otherwise.
  • Label the vial with date and concentration (e.g., “5 mg in 2 mL = 2.5 mg/mL”).
  • Minimize temperature cycling; keep out of light.
How long does reconstituted peptide last?

General practice: use within 28 days when mixed with BaW and refrigerated. Actual stability varies by peptide and manufacturer—follow your product’s guidance.

Can I use sterile water instead of bacteriostatic water?

You can, but SWFI lacks a preservative. It’s typically intended for single-use; discard the remainder to reduce contamination risk. For multi-dose vials, BaW is generally preferred (follow your clinician’s instructions).

Injection route and needle size?

Follow your clinician’s directions. Commonly subcutaneous with 29–31G insulin syringes; intramuscular protocols vary. Site rotation and sterile technique are essential.

How do I avoid contamination?
  • Alcohol swab stoppers and skin; let dry.
  • Use new sterile needles/syringes each time.
  • Do not leave needles in the vial. Cap promptly; refrigerate.
Disposal of sharps and leftover solution

Use a sharps container for needles/syringes. Follow local regulations or pharmacy take-back for disposal. Don’t pour leftovers down the drain.

Traveling with reconstituted peptides

Keep labeled, in original vials where possible, with cooling pack if needed. Check airline and destination rules. Carry a copy of your prescription or clinician note if applicable.
